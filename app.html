🤝 The 5 Ways to Reach Physicians Legally
All five methods below are fully permitted under PHIPA and CPO advertising guidelines. No referral fees — relationships only.
1. In-Person Drop-In Visit Most Effective
Visit the clinic during non-peak hours (11 am–1 pm or after 4 pm). Ask for the clinic manager or office coordinator first — not the doctor directly.

What to bring:
  • Referral pads (pre-printed with your clinic info)
  • Brochures listing all services (physio, MVA, WSIB, concussion, pediatric, etc.)
  • Business cards with your direct line + fax number
  • A one-page "services overview" sheet

What to say:
"Hi, my name is [Your Name] from Alleviate Physiotherapy — we're located at [Address], about [X] minutes from here. We offer physiotherapy, acupuncture, chiropractic, RMT, concussion rehab, pediatric physio, and custom orthotics. We have same-week availability and send detailed progress reports back to referring physicians after every 4 sessions. Would it be okay to leave some referral pads and information for your doctors?"
Follow up: Return within 4–6 weeks. Bring coffee or treats for the front desk — small gestures build lasting goodwill.
2. Email Outreach
Find the clinic email on their website or call to ask. Use the templates in the Doctor Network page. Send a professional introduction.

  • Subject line should be specific: "Physiotherapy Referral Partnership — [Your Clinic Name]"
  • Keep it under 200 words — doctors are busy
  • Offer something specific: "We guarantee appointments within 48–72 hours"
  • Follow up with a phone call 3–5 business days later
  • Attach a one-page services PDF if possible
3. Phone + Fax
Call and ask for the Practice Manager or Referral Coordinator. Many Ontario medical offices still use fax — fax a one-page introduction with your referral pad details.

Phone script:
"Hi, I'm calling from Alleviate Physiotherapy in Mississauga. I was hoping to connect with your practice manager about a referral partnership — we offer physiotherapy, MVA, WSIB, concussion rehab, and several other services with very quick intake times. Would it be okay to fax over our information, or could I schedule a brief 10-minute call?"
Fax tip: Include your fax number prominently on your referral pad so completed pads get sent back to you efficiently.
4. Lunch & Learn Highest ROI Long-Term
Offer to bring lunch for the clinic staff and give a 15–20 minute presentation about your services. This builds the deepest relationships.

  • Contact the clinic manager to book a date
  • Bring food for 5–15 people (budget $100–200)
  • Present: who you are, what you treat, what makes you different, how the referral process works
  • Leave referral pads, brochures, business cards with every staff member
  • Offer to answer specific patient case questions
5. CPSO Directory Search — Find Any Licensed Physician in Ontario
The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario publishes a public register of all licensed physicians. Use it to find doctors by specialty and city.

  1. Go to cpso.on.ca/Public-Register
  2. Search by City: Mississauga or Brampton
  3. Filter by Specialty: Family Medicine, Orthopedic Surgery, Neurology, etc.
  4. Find their practice address and clinic phone number
  5. Add them to your Doctor Network above
🔒 PHIPA Compliance — What You Need to Know
Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA, 2004) governs how patient information is handled. Here's what matters for your referral network.
✅ You CAN
  • Collect doctors' business contact info (name, phone, email, address — this is NOT personal health information)
  • Receive a patient referral from a physician under the "circle of care" — no extra consent needed
  • Share patient progress info back with the referring physician (this is encouraged)
  • Store referral tracking data using patient codes/initials in this platform
  • Market your clinic professionally to physicians
  • Discuss services and capabilities with referring doctors
❌ You CANNOT
  • Store full patient names + diagnosis + full treatment records in an unsecured web platform
  • Share a patient's health information with any provider outside their circle of care without written consent
  • Use patient health data for marketing purposes without consent
  • Give patient information to lawyers/insurers without the patient's signed consent (FSRA Form)
  • Disclose patient information over unsecured email without encryption
ℹ️ Circle of Care (PHIPA s.38)
When a physician refers a patient to you for treatment, both of you are in that patient's "circle of care." You can share clinical information with the referring physician without additional consent — this is not only permitted but expected. Sending progress reports is how you build the referral relationship.
🏥 CPO Standards — College of Physiotherapists of Ontario
Key standards from the College of Physiotherapists of Ontario that apply to building your referral network.
Standard 3 — Communication & Collaborative Care Critical for Referrals
CPO requires physiotherapists to communicate appropriately with other health professionals involved in a patient's care.

Practical implication: Send a brief progress note or discharge summary back to the referring physician after every 4–6 sessions, and upon discharge. This is what converts a one-time referral into an ongoing relationship. It shows you're a reliable partner.

What to include in your progress note:
  • Patient's initial presentation and objective findings
  • Treatment approach and goals
  • Progress to date and current functional status
  • Next steps or recommendations
CPO Advertising Guidelines — What's Allowed
The CPO permits professional marketing and advertising. It must be:
  • Truthful — Do not claim services you don't offer or outcomes you can't guarantee
  • Not misleading — No exaggerated claims about results
  • Dignified — Maintain professional standards in all outreach
  • Compliant with RHPA — No prohibited advertising under the Regulated Health Professions Act
All five outreach methods above (drop-in, email, phone, lunch & learn, CPSO search) are fully permitted.
❌ Fee-Splitting is Strictly Prohibited
  • You cannot pay physicians per referral (cash, gifts, commissions, or any financial benefit)
  • You cannot receive payment from specialists for sending patients to them
  • Violates CPO Standards AND the Regulated Health Professions Act (RHPA)
  • Can result in suspension of registration
  • Build relationships through excellent care and communication — not payments
📝 Using Your Referral Pads
Referral pads are one of the most effective tools for building physician referrals. Here's how to deploy them effectively.
What Your Referral Pad Should Include
  • Your clinic name, address, and logo — prominent at the top
  • Phone number — for the patient/clinic to call
  • Fax number — so the completed form gets sent back to you
  • Services checklist — checkboxes for: Physiotherapy, MVA, WSIB, Concussion, Pediatric Physio, Acupuncture, Chiropractic, RMT, Naturopathy, Osteopathy, Custom Orthotics, Custom Braces
  • Patient info fields — Name, DOB, contact number
  • Diagnosis / Reason for Referral — free text line
  • Urgency — Routine / Urgent / ASAP checkbox
  • Physician name + signature + date
  • Insurance type — Extended Health / MVA (claim #) / WSIB (claim #) / Private
How to Distribute Them
  • Leave 10–20 pads per clinic during your drop-in visit
  • Return every 4–6 weeks to replenish and check in
  • Ask the front desk admin to keep them visible at the check-out desk
  • Follow up: "Have you had any patients who might benefit from physio?" is a natural conversation starter
  • Track which clinics you've dropped pads at — add a note in each doctor's card
